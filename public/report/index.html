What this is

A four-year audit of every support thread in the Supabase community Discord. Each thread was scored for frustration and grouped by topic, reply coverage was measured for all 41,392, and the 96 most difficult threads were read in full to find the root cause and whether replies helped.

Who it's for

Support, docs, and product teams deciding where to invest next: self-service recovery, response capacity, an education push, or new runbooks. Every claim traces back to real threads in the data.

How to read it

What's rising and Who answers are the two headline stories — demand and supply. Rising threads shows what those threads say. Doc work and Fixes are the action list. The rest is reference.

01 · What's rising

Three areas are growing against an 18% decline.

The chart compares the last six months with the six months before, per product area, adjusted for the forum-wide decline. A score of 1.0 means the area moved with the forum as a whole. Above 1.0 means it grew despite the decline. Three areas clearly rise: Billing/Quotas (1.74), Network/DNS (1.63), and Outage/Status (1.58) — with Dashboard/Access (1.15) a smaller fourth.

Relative change by product area, last 6 months vs prior 6 · vertical line = forum-wide baseline (1.0) · green = growing · gray = shrinking with the forum

What this means The growing areas are not documentation problems. Auth — the largest documentation-gap area at 12,848 threads — is shrinking with the forum (0.90). What's growing is where the platform itself breaks: billing state, project status, and network reachability. The fallers are instructive too: Edge Functions is down 81% and Auth 71% from their 2023 peaks, against 56% for the forum — a gradual slide consistent with compounding doc and SDK fixes, not one big change. The forum itself peaked in Q3 2023; the recent −18% is the tail of a three-year decline.

What changed in August 2025: the tag, more than the volume

outage-tagged threads per month, last 18 months

Two different stories. The outage tag steps from ~5–25 uses/month to 58–109/month in Aug 2025 — but the tag only existed from 2024 and moderators clearly began applying it consistently then. Under the keyword classifier, outage volume rises only modestly over the same window (~1.3–1.5×). The step is mostly a moderation-behaviour signal, not a reliability one.

02 · Who answers

Nearly everything gets a reply — one person writes a quarter of them.

Only 11.6% of the 41,392 threads have zero replies, and on the threads whose replies we read in full, just 2.3% of the rest are the asker talking to themselves — so about 14% never hear from anyone else. Answering has been getting better: the last full quarter sits at 2.5%. What has not improved is who does it. One community moderator, garyaustin, has written 27% of all 270,020 replies — steady at 24–28% every quarter for two years. The top 12 repliers hold about 41%, and none identify as Supabase staff — the biggest answerers describe themselves as volunteer moderators.

Correction, 2026-08-10 An earlier version of this report said 53% of threads never receive a reply. That came from responder_count, a field our own reply backfill populates — and the backfill never reached 40% of the corpus, which then read as silence. Discord's own thread metadata (message_count, which excludes the opening post) matches the fetched reply count on 98.5% of the 27,622 threads where both exist, and puts the real figure at 11.6%. Every no-reply number below is recomputed from that field.

What this means Coverage is the best it has ever been — 2.5% of last quarter's threads went unanswered, against 17.8% in mid-2024 — because volume fell faster than the answerers' capacity. 03 · Documentation gaps

Auth, Storage, Realtime, and Edge Functions need the most doc work.

Each area is scored for frustration (negative language, repeated failed attempts) and for how often people blame the docs, ask how-to questions, or stay confused. This is the documentation view — a different signal from the rising areas in section 01. Auth/JWT/OAuth leads on volume (12,848 threads) and frustration (0.76) — though its doc-blame rate is average, so the lead comes from size, and its genuinely under-served corners are SSR session handling (11.9% no-reply, n=1,708) and OAuth redirect config (13.7%, n=846) — both above the 11.6% forum average.

05 · New vocabulary

The forum's newest words are support-ticket words.

Terms whose frequency grew the most in the last six months. They cluster around account restrictions, billing lifecycle, and incident recovery — users come to the forum to learn the words that get a support ticket noticed.

Fastest-growing terms, last 6 months vs prior 6 · longer bar = larger increase

What this means restricted · locked · banned · stuck pausing · grace period · recovery · 522 · egress — every top term is an account or platform state a user got stuck in. Threads titled "UPGRADE STUCK" or "Project stuck Pausing" are users repeating the keywords they've learned catch the support filter.

06 · Reply triage

96 difficult threads: two-thirds bugs or outages, one in five resolved.

The catch The threads that do get resolved are almost all user misconfiguration that fixes itself. Bug and outage threads are essentially never resolved by replies — the only path is opening a support ticket and waiting days.

resolution here is judged from reading the replies, not taken from the forum's own “resolved” marker — that marker is an optional self-assessment the asker may never apply, so any tag-derived resolution rate is a floor, not a measurement.

What this means The fastest-growing part of the forum has a 21% reply-resolution rate, concentrated in self-fixing issues. The forum is absorbing incident-management work that the product and support process are not.

Seven patterns, confirmed across all six review passes

each appeared independently in at least 3 of 6 batches
  • P1Platform-side state gets stuck, and there is no self-service recovery. Projects stuck pausing for days, restores reporting "no backups found", stuck upgrades, resizes offline for 24h, ghost projects. Every one ends the same way: open a ticket and wait.
  • P2Support response lags, and the status page disagrees with reality. Pro customers report production down for hours to weeks while the status page reads "All Systems Operational". Users learn which ticket keywords get a faster response.
  • P3The dashboard fails exactly when the project is degraded. Restarts fail, the network-bans page won't load, key rotation deadlocks — the control plane is unusable at the moment it's needed most.
  • P4*.supabase.co is unreachable from specific networks. A Brazil ISP block, Mullvad VPN timeouts, Vercel sin1 DNS failures — all misattributed to project outages.
  • P5Key rotation has a security gap. Revoking the HS256 signing secret still honours the legacy apikey path for 12+ hours; a revoked secret still authorizes.
  • P6Billing and ban lifecycle is opaque. Silent bans, no published grace period, and the Pro upgrade doesn't auto-apply the included micro compute.
  • P7Resource limits are hit without warning. Disk full (pg_wal), row limits, pool saturation — no early warning, experienced as "my project died".

Method

All 41,392 threads and 270,020 replies were pulled from the Supabase community Discord (2022-08-11 → 2026-08-08). Frustration is measured from the text itself: sentiment, repeated failed attempts, doc-blame, and how-to/confusion density — not response time, which measures responsiveness rather than difficulty. Trend compares the last six months with the prior six, adjusted for the forum-wide decline (0.82). The triage read up to 6 replies × 500 characters in each of 96 high-frustration threads from the last 12 months. No-reply rates come from Discord's own message_count on each thread (which excludes the opening post), not from our fetched replies — the fetch covers 60% of the corpus, and treating the un-fetched remainder as unanswered is what produced the retracted 53% figure. Responder concentration is computed from the 270,020 fetched replies, whose authorship is unaffected by that coverage gap. Every headline figure was independently re-derived by adversarial fact-check agents (see fact-check.md); per-feature levels inherit a noisy keyword classifier (~50–80% precision by area), so treat single-area absolutes as approximate and trends as the reliable signal.
